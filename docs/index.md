# How to Answer "Walk Me Through Your Current Role" in an Interview

"Walk me through your current role" is often the second question after "tell me about yourself." It's a chance to show scope, ownership and impact, but many candidates read out their job description. A better answer gives the interviewer a clear picture of what you own, how you work and what changed because you were there.

## What the interviewer wants to learn

- **Scope:** your team, product and who you work with.
- **Ownership:** which parts are truly yours, not the team's.
- **Impact:** results you can point to, ideally with numbers.
- **Relevance:** how this experience maps to the role you're interviewing for.

## Structure your answer in four parts

1. **One-line summary:** title, team and the mission of the team.
2. **Day-to-day:** the two or three responsibilities that take most of your time.
3. **A highlight:** one project with a measurable result.
4. **Bridge:** why the next step you want is this role.

## Example answer

> "I'm a backend engineer on a five-person payments team at a mid-size e-commerce company. Day to day I own our refund and dispute services, review most of the team's API changes, and take on-call one week a month. My biggest project this year was moving refunds to an event-driven queue, which cut failed refunds by sixty percent and saved support about ten hours a week. I'd like to go deeper into distributed systems at larger scale, which is a big part of why this platform role interests me."

## Common mistakes

- Listing every task instead of picking the important ones.
- Using internal acronyms the interviewer won't know.
- Talking only about the team and never saying "I."
- Running past two minutes. Keep it tight and let them ask follow-ups.

## Rehearse until it feels natural

Write your answer as bullets, not a script, and practice it out loud a few times. Expect follow-ups like "what would you change about your role?" or "what's the hardest part?"
